## Patterns (what transfers to the other items)

1. **Absence claims were grepped for the literal phrase, not the idea.** C34/S3 declared "no human in the loop" absent after searching four spellings of one phrase; the subject says the same thing in its own words ("without a human co-pilot", "acts autonomously", "without human involvement") on three pages the pass had already fetched. Every absence claim in the other items should be re-run with a synonym list, not a spelling list. This is the method-rules "over-correction against the subject" corollary, and it changed a headline S-table verdict.
2. **Quotes are real; citations drift.** Not one fabricated quote in 62 claims — every verbatim string in the appendix exists somewhere. But three quotes carry the wrong URL (two HN comments to the wrong story; Google Brain to the launch post instead of the team page). A reader who follows the link will not find the text. Fact-checks should verify the *page*, not just the string.
3. **"Unknown" used where the source answers.** The Sep-22 string was labelled unknown-meaning and parked in open-verification; it is an HTML head comment three lines into the file the pass had already fetched. Check the raw file around a puzzling string before opening an O-item.
