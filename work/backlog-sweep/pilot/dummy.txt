The harbour town of Velmora sits on a narrow bay, and its fish market opens before dawn each morning. Traders arrive with crates of ice, and the first boats usually dock by five. Prices are chalked on slate boards that hang from hooks above each stall. Regular buyers know which stalls carry the best mackerel, which carry shellfish, and which are best avoided on hot days. The market closes around noon, when the remaining stock is sold cheaply to restaurants or carried to the smokehouse at the end of the pier. Gulls gather on the roofs and wait. By afternoon the stalls are hosed down, the ice melts into the gutters, and the square fills with children on bicycles. In winter the schedule shifts later because the boats go out in daylight only, and some stalls close for months. A small museum near the pier displays old nets, tide charts, and photographs of storms that damaged the breakwater. Visitors who stay overnight can take a ferry across the bay to the lighthouse, which is open on weekends from spring until autumn.
